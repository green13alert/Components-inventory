## Circuit

The signal map is unchanged: pan D9, tilt D10, joystick A0, A1, and D2. Servo red wires are not connected to Uno 5 V. Grounds are common.

- Conflicts: Tower Pro’s operating-current range can exceed the 3 A supply when both servos are at the high end. Adafruit 368 has no published current rating. Clamping Dupont pins under its screws is not the connection the terminal is specified for.
- Unverified: stall current of the SG90 used in this mount, temperature of the 368 joint, and whether a 3 A supply holds 5 V while both servos move.

## Candidates that were not selected

1. **Uno 5 V or a computer USB port.** Rejected. Arduino does not rate that pin for this load, and Tower Pro says to use a separate supply. One servo at 2 A is already beyond a normal USB port.
2. **Keep the 3 A adapter and the 368, and call the Dupont clamp acceptable.** Rejected. The current rating of that joint is not published, and 3 A is below the top of the stated per-servo range for two servos.
3. **A breadboard regulator module such as an MB102.** Not selected. Those boards commonly use a regulator around 1 A, which is below one servo at the top of Tower Pro’s range. Breadboard contacts are also a poor high-current joint.
4. **An RC BEC with a servo plug.** Plausible and common, but no specific BEC with a published continuous rating above 4 A was chosen. Selecting one without that sheet would be a guess.

## Verification required before a new power path

- Running and stall current for the exact SG90s on this bracket, both moving.
- A connector or terminal whose manufacturer publishes a current above that total, mating with the JR plug or with the servo conductor. Not a Dupont pin under a screw.
- A 5 V supply whose continuous rating exceeds that total.
- A bench check that the Uno does not reset and the joint does not heat.

Until those exist, slug 18 stays unpublished. Existing progress rows are left in place.
